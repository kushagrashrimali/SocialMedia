# WYSTAK launch reel v5: merchant script, "40 soundbox beeps" (30s, 9:16)

Supersedes v4. Stamp cards were never part of Indian retail, so the history now runs only through things every Indian shop owner has lived: the kirana wala who knew you, "Sir, mobile number?", credit card points, and the UPI soundbox.

## Why this one can travel

- **Hook (scored 81 / STRONG on the reel hook scorer):** a concrete number and a sting in the first 3 seconds. Every shop owner has a regular they can picture.
- **The soundbox "beep" is the motif.** Every shop in India has one and every viewer knows the sound. It opens the film (40 beeps), lands the turn ("Beep. That's it."), and pays off at the end ("every beep brings your regular back"). Sound people recognise in the first second stops the scroll, and the word gets repeated in the comments.
- **Nostalgia that's shareable:** "Your dad's kirana wala knew your name. Free dhaniya, every single time." This is the line people tag their family on.
- **"Sir, mobile number?"** is a quote every Indian has heard at a billing counter, so it lands instantly.
- **The quotable line:** *India digitised the payment. Not the relationship.* (pitch deck, slide 2). It works as the reel's caption, a carousel cover and a comment-bait line.
- **The loop:** the last spoken beat echoes the hook's "regular" and "beep", so the replay feels intentional.

## Claims check

- "40 times this year" is an illustrative regular, not a statistic. It's said as "your regular", so it reads as a scenario.
- The soundbox, store and credit cards are all generic: no Paytm, PhonePe or bank branding on screen.
- The rewards are the shop's own points on its own pass. The demo shows the real flow (customer pays, staff scan the pass); Wystak doesn't reward the UPI payment itself.
- No app, updates within seconds, and Apple and Google Wallet all come from the pitch deck. No prices or market numbers.
- Fictional merchant: Paper Crane Coffee.
