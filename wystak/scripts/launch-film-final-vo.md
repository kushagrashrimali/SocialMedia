# WYSTAK launch film: final voiceover

Supersedes v1–v5. Built from the project context, the pitch deck, the reference reel, and the `product-launch-motion` (story and truth), `short-form-video` (hook and loop) and `ig-reel` (hook scoring, beat timing) skills.

## 1. Creative concept

**"The free coffee you forgot."** Almost everyone has a reward they've already earned and can't find. It might be on a card in a drawer, behind a phone number given at a counter, inside an app deleted for space, or buried in WhatsApp under the good-morning forwards. The film opens on that small, universal moment of recognition, then turns it around for the business: the café thinks loyalty doesn't work, when really its customers just can't find it. The insight is that **loyalty never had a home**. The phone already has a place for things people can't afford to lose, the wallet, so Wystak puts loyalty there. The film pays off its own hook ("That free coffee? Now you know exactly where it is."). It then shows the merchant what they get, regulars who finally have names, and closes on a thought bigger than the software: *your customers were always loyal; loyalty just needed somewhere to live.*

Other angles I explored and dropped:
- **Physical → digital → wallet history (v3/v4):** clear, but it's a history lesson before it's a feeling.
- **The 40 soundbox beeps (v5):** sharp, but it's merchant-only and leans on UPI, which Wystak doesn't touch.
- **The "another app" problem:** true, but it's one symptom, not the story.
- **The regular who quietly stopped coming:** emotional, but on its own it reads like a churn dashboard.

The "forgotten reward" angle holds all of these inside one human moment, and it has a built-in payoff line.

## 4. Delivery direction

- **Personality:** a founder explaining something they've thought about for a long time. They're amused by the problem, sure of the answer, and never selling. Think of a friend across the table, not a stage.
- **Pace:** an unhurried conversational pace, around 150–155 wpm, with full stops treated as real breaths.
- **Hook (0:00–0:05):** light, almost conspiratorial, a small smile in the voice. "You just don't remember where" lands softly; don't make it a punchline.
- **The "maybe" list (0:05–0:20):** gentle rhythm with each line a fraction quicker than the last, building recognition. "Under the good morning forwards" gets a dry, knowing tone; it should get a smile, not a laugh.
- **"You still go to that café every Sunday":** warmer and slower. This is the human line.
- **The turn ("It works… It just never had a home."):** slow down. A real beat after "It works." Lean on **home**.
- **Insight → reveal:** "The wallet." is said simply, as if it were obvious all along. Pause before "This is Wystak." Say the name plainly and confidently, with no announcer lift.
- **Product lines:** crisp and matter-of-fact. Short sentences, no excitement. The confidence comes from how little needs explaining.
- **"That free coffee?":** the smile comes back. It's the callback.
- **"…regulars finally have names":** lean on **names**. "And who quietly stopped" is quieter, almost an aside.
- **Ending:** slowest part of the film. "Your customers were always loyal." Breath. "Loyalty just needed somewhere to live." Hold, then "Wystak. All your passes. One stack." even and settled, falling at the end.
- **Avoid:** an upward sales lilt, punching every line, a motivational tone, and rushing the pauses to fit time.
- **ElevenLabs:** a warm, natural voice (Indian-English or neutral). Multilingual v2: Stability 50–60 · Similarity 75 · Style 10–20 · Speaker boost on · Speed 0.95–1.0. Export WAV.

### Weakest part, improved once

The first draft's business beat was "And the café finally sees who keeps coming back." That is vague, and it's the one line meant to sell to the actual buyer. The final version is:

> **And for the café, regulars finally have names. Who keeps coming back. And who quietly stopped.**

It's concrete and true: enrolment captures a first name, and the dashboard shows returning and lapsed customers, as in pitch deck slide 3, "who comes back, who stopped, and when". It also echoes the merchant pain from v5 (a regular you only know as a payment). It gives the business owner a reason to care without turning into a feature list. **Launch-video suitability goes from 7 to 8.**

### Truth check

- No statistics, prices, traction or customer stories.
- Every product claim is supported: no app, scan to add the pass, Apple Wallet and Google Wallet ("the wallet", never Apple alone), points on the lock screen within seconds of a scan, and a merchant view of returning and lapsed customers.
- "Boarding passes. Tickets." describes what phone wallets already hold in general. It doesn't claim Wystak issues them.
- "Pass" is used for the wallet object; "card" only means the physical one in the drawer.
- The ₹1,500 → 150 points → ₹100 off example is left out of the voiceover, because one rule set would read as the product's only rule set. It can appear on screen during the demo as an example the merchant sets.
